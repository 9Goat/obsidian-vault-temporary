
# words meaning

- **Upmarket** is an adjective used to describe **goods, services, or places that are relatively expensive and intended for people who are wealthy or have high social status.**
- In marketing, a **segment** is a group of customers who share one or more **common characteristics** and have similar **product needs** or **responses to a marketing action**.
- A **slogan** (or **tagline**) in marketing is a **short, memorable, and catchy phrase** used in advertising to encapsulate a company's **brand promise, product features, or competitive position**.


# phrases meanings

- **Brand stretching**, also known as **brand extension**, is a marketing strategy where a company uses the established **brand name** of an existing product with a strong reputation on a new product in a **different category**.
- A **market challenger** is a company that holds the **second-largest market share** in an industry, often slightly behind the **market leader**.
- **Product endorsement** is a marketing technique where a company uses a well-known person, organization, or fictional character to publicly recommend or vouch for a product or service.
- **Range of products**, often used interchangeably with **product line** or **product assortment**, refers to the complete **set of all products and services** offered for sale by a single company.

# present simple


The Present Simple describes **facts, habits, and fixed schedules**. It focuses on actions that are **permanent, repeated, or generally true**.

### Form

- **Affirmative:** Base form of the verb (add −s or −es for third-person singular: _she writes_).
- **Negative:** Subject + _do/does not_ + base verb.
- **Question:** _Do/Does_ + subject + base verb.

| **Use**                    | **Explanation**                                                      | **Example**                       |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Habits & Routines**      | Actions done regularly or habitually.                                | I **drink** coffee every morning. |
| **Facts & General Truths** | Statements that are universally or permanently true.                 | The sun **rises** in the east.    |
| **Fixed Schedules**        | Timetables for public transport, movies, etc.                        | The train **leaves** at 7:00 AM.  |
| **States**                 | Verbs that describe permanent states (e.g., _be, know, like, want_). | She **lives** in Rome.            |

# present continuous

The Present Continuous (or Present Progressive) describes **actions happening right now** or **temporary events**. It focuses on actions that are **in progress** and **incomplete**.

### Form

- **Affirmative:** Subject + _to be_ (am, is, are) + verb + _-ing_.
- **Negative:** Subject + _to be_ + _not_ + verb + _-ing_.
- **Question:** _To be_ + subject + verb + _-ing_.

| **Use**                              | **Explanation**                                                                       | **Example**                                |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Action at the Moment of Speaking** | Something happening exactly now.                                                      | I **am writing** an email right now.       |
| **Temporary Situations**             | An action happening around the present time, but not necessarily at this very moment. | He **is studying** Spanish this semester.  |
| **Changing Situations**              | Things that are developing or changing.                                               | The climate **is getting** warmer.         |
| **Annoying Habits**                  | Used with _always_ to complain about a frequent, irritating action.                   | You **are always leaving** your shoes out! |

# differences between both

|**Feature**|**Present Simple**|**Present Continuous**|
|---|---|---|
|**Nature of Action**|Permanent, Repeated, Factual|Temporary, In progress, Incomplete|
|**Time Focus**|General Time (Every day, always)|Specific Time (Now, currently, this week)|
|**Key Verbs**|Usually includes stative verbs (know, like)|Rarely uses stative verbs; uses action verbs|

You determine if a verb is not normally found in the continuous present by checking if it describes a **state** rather than an **action** or an ongoing activity.
