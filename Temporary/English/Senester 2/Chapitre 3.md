


# Phrases explanation

## point of sales advertisements

**Point of sale advertising** (often abbreviated as **POS advertising**) refers to promotional materials placed exactly where a customer is about to pay for a product.

## out door advertisements

**Outdoor advertisement** (also known as **Out-of-Home** or **OOH** advertising) refers to any type of advertising that reaches consumers while they are outside of their homes.

Unlike a TV ad or a social media post that you see on a personal device, outdoor ads are "public" and usually target people who are traveling, shopping, or waiting in transit.

## shop till you drop

The phrase **"to shop till you drop"** is a very common idiom in English. It is used to describe a person who goes shopping for a very long time until they are physically exhausted.

## by and large

**"by and large"** is a common English expression that means **"mostly," "generally,"** or **"on the whole."**

It is used when you want to describe a situation that is true in most cases, even if there are a few small exceptions.

## junk mail

this name is given to all the ad sales and prize offers and get sent to our mails/letterboxes without you having requested them

# words explanations

## endorses

**to endorse** means to give public support or approval to a product, usually by a famous person, an expert, or an official organization.

When a celebrity **endorses** a product, they are essentially saying, "I use this, and I think you should too."

## persuades

**to persuade** means to convince someone to believe something or to take a specific action (usually buying a product).

It is the core mission of any **advertising agency**. If an ad doesn't **persuade** the customer, the **sales figures** will not grow.

## hoardings

**hoardings** (mainly used in British and Indian English) refer to the **large wooden or metal boards** used to display advertisements outdoors.

In American English, these are almost always called **billboards**.

## slogan

**slogan** is a short, catchy, and memorable phrase used in advertising to identify a product or a brand.

The goal of a slogan is to "stick" in the customer's mind so that they immediately think of the company when they hear those specific words.

## subverting

**Subverting** means to go against what people usually expect or to "break the rules" of how things are normally done.

**Example**: If a company usually charges a lot of money for a phone, but a new company gives phones away for free, they are **subverting** the normal market.

##  subvertising

**Subvertising** is a mix of two words: **Subvert** (to go against) and **Advertising**. It is when people take a famous ad and change it to make a joke or a point.

**Example**: Imagine a famous soda ad that says _"Taste the Happiness."_ A **subvertisement** might change the words to _"Taste the Sugar"_ and show a picture of a tooth falling out.
