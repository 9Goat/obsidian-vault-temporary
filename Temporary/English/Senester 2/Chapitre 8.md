## Short list candidates

To **shortlist candidates** means to choose a small group of the best applicants from a large pool of resumes. These are the top people who will move on to the interview stage.

## probationary period

A **probationary period** is a trial period (usually 3 to 6 months) when a new employee starts a job. During this time, the company watches their performance to see if they are a good fit before making the job permanent.

## Covering letter

A **covering letter** (also called a cover letter) is a one-page document you send _with_ your resume when applying for a job. This is a personal letter written in full sentences. It connects the dots for the employer, explaining _why_ your skills match their specific job and _why_ you want to work there.

## Psychometric test

A standardized test used by employers to evaluate your personality traits, thinking style, and mental aptitude to see if you are a good fit for a job.

## Interview panel

A panel interview is  ==a hiring format where two to five interviewers evaluate a single candidate in one session==.

## HeadHunter

A **headhunter** is a recruiter who finds the perfect person for a specific job, usually for high-level or specialized positions, and convinces them to leave their current company.

## Applicant

An **applicant** is a person who officially asks for a job or a position, by filling out a form or sending in a resume.

## Vacancy

A **vacancy** is an unfilled or available job position within a company. It means there is an opening, and the company is actively looking for someone to hire.

## reference

A **reference** is a statement or letter from a previous employer or colleague that describes your work ethic, skills, and character.

New employers ask for references to double-check that you are honest, reliable, and actually good at your job.

## CV

**CV** stands for _Curriculum Vitae_ (often called a **resume**). It is a short, structured document that lists your work history, education, skills, and achievements.

You send it to employers when you apply for a job so they can quickly see if you have the right background.

## Remuneration

**Remuneration** is the total amount of money and benefits an employee receives for doing their work.

It includes your basic monthly salary, plus extras like cash bonuses, health insurance, or a company car.

# Reported speech

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**1.Identify the question type :**Determines if you need a connecting word or an 'if/whether' connector.

Check whether the original question is a **Yes/No question** or a **Wh- question** (using _who, what, where, when, why, how_).

  

- **Yes/No Example:** "Did you finish the report?"
    
      
    
- **Wh- Example:** "Where is the meeting held?"
    
      
    

**2.Choose an appropriate reporting verb :**Sets up the reported structure.

Select a reporting verb in the past tense. Common choices include:

  

- **Ask** (most common)
    
      
    
- **Inquire / Want to know / Wonder** (more formal or reflective)
    
      
    

_Example:_ "She asked..." or "They wanted to know..."

  

**3.Insert the connector :**Connects the main clause to the reported question.

- **For Yes/No questions:** Add **if** or **whether**.
    
      
    
- **For Wh- questions:** Keep the original **Wh- word** (_where, when, why, how, etc._).
    
      
    

**4.Change the word order to statement form :**Crucial rule: remove auxiliary verbs used for questions.

Reorder the clause into normal subject-verb structure: **[Subject] + [Verb]**.

  

- **Remove** question auxiliaries like _do, does,_ or _did_.
    
      
    
- **Do not** invert the subject and auxiliary verb (e.g., change _is he_ to _he was_).
    
      
    

**5.Apply tense backshifting and pronoun/time adjustments :**Aligns the reported statement with past time context.

- **Backshift the verb tense** one step into the past (unless the statement remains universally true or the discussion was just now).
    
      
    
- **Adjust pronouns** (_I/you_ $\rightarrow$ _he/she/they_) and **time/place references** (_now_ $\rightarrow$ _then_, _today_ $\rightarrow$ _that day_).
    
      
    
- End the sentence with a **period (.)**, not a question mark.
    
      
    

## Core Rules for Reporting Questions

### 1. Grammatical Word Order (No Question Forms)

In direct speech, questions use inverted word order or auxiliary verbs (_Do, Does, Did_). In reported speech, the clause becomes a standard noun clause.

  

|**Direct Question**|**Correct Reported Speech**|**Incorrect (Common Error)**|
|---|---|---|
|"Where **do you** live?"|He asked me where **I lived**.|_He asked me where did I live._|
|"Are **you** ready?"|She asked if **I was** ready.|_She asked was I ready._|

### 2. Tense Backshifting Rules

When the reporting verb is in the past tense (e.g., _asked_), backshift the original verb tenses as follows:

  

|**Direct Tense**|**Reported Tense**|**Direct Example**|**Reported Example**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Present Simple**|**Past Simple**|"What time **is** it?"|He asked what time it **was**.|
|**Present Continuous**|**Past Continuous**|"Are you **working**?"|She asked if I **was working**.|
|**Past Simple / Present Perfect**|**Past Perfect**|"Did you **see** him?"|They asked if I **had seen** him.|
|**Will**|**Would**|"Will you **attend**?"|He asked if I **would attend**.|
|**Can**|**Could**|"Where can we **park**?"|She asked where they **could park**.|

### 3. Pronoun & Context Shift Rules

|**Direct Element**|**Reported Element**|**Direct Example**|**Reported Example**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**you**|**I / he / she / we / they**|"How are **you**?"|He asked how **I** was.|
|**here**|**there**|"Is the file **here**?"|She asked if the file was **there**.|
|**tomorrow**|**the next day / following day**|"Will you call **tomorrow**?"|He asked if I would call **the next day**.|
|**now**|**then / at that moment**|"What are you doing **now**?"|She asked what I was doing **then**.|

## Direct vs. Reported Examples Summary

- **Wh- Question:**
    
      
    - _Direct:_ "Why did the client cancel the order?"
        
          
        
    - _Reported:_ She asked **why the client had canceled** the order.
        
          
        
- **Yes/No Question:**
    
      
    - _Direct:_ "Have you reviewed the budget?"
        
          
        
    - _Reported:_ He inquired **if I had reviewed** the budget.
        
          
        
- **Modal Question:**
    
      
    - _Direct:_ "Can we adjust the timeline?"
        
          
        
    - _Reported:_ They asked **whether they could adjust** the timeline.
