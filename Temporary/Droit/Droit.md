# Computer crimes

the tunisian penal code lists 3 computer crimes which are:

## 1. Fraudulent/frauduleux access

### Definitions

- *According to **Article 199 bis of the Criminal Code**, it constitutes a **computer crime** to **fraudulently access or maintain** all or part of an **automated data processing system**.

- An **automated data processing system** is defined as "**any system composed of one or more memory units, data software, input-output devices, and connections that contribute to a specific result**."

- **Spyware** is generally defined, in the broad sense, as **software designed to collect data from a computer or other device and transfer it to a third party without the computer owner's consent or knowledge**.

### How does the law handle this crime?



 **Unlike French law, Tunisian law requires the protected nature of the system** to be established for the law to apply. This means that for an offense to be prosecuted, the automated data processing system itself must be shown to have been protected (e.g., with passwords, firewalls, encryption)..
 
  In all cases, proof of such an offense may result from **the insertion of a spy file (such as cookies, Trojan horses, adware programs, etc.) or a pirated connection aimed at remotely interrogating a system, or calling a program or consulting a file without authorization.**

In all cases, this offence is punishable by a fine of one thousand dinars and imprisonment of two months to one year, or one of the two penalties only.

## 2. Attacks on the automated system

from the same article is it very similar to the crime above except that this focuses on **DAMAGES** to the system rather than just the acting of accessing it

### Definitions

-  According to **Article 199 bis of the Penal Code**, any **fraudulent access causing the alteration or destruction of an automated processing system** constitutes a **computer crime**.

### The 2 types of attacks on a system

#### The destruction of the functioning of the automated system


These actions are defined as **changing the functionalities of the system**. This means causing the system to produce an outcome different from what is expected.

Specific examples of these actions include:

- **Blocking** the call of a program or a file.
- **Changing** one of the elements of the system.
- **Inserting viruses or logic bombs.**
- **interfering the system** by destroying files or backup programs.
- **Cluttering the memory capacity.**

#### Fraudulently Introducing Data


The text focuses on the act of **fraudulently introducing data** into an **automated processing system**. This includes any **manipulation of data**—like introducing, deleting, modifying, or distorting it—that alters the system's program, processing, or transmission method.

Examples of this crime include:

- Modifying the pages of a web server.
- Differentiating (changing) a web server's address in search engines.
- Programming false bank cards.

The penalty for this offense is **five years' imprisonment and a fine of five thousand dinars**.

## La modification de document informatise or electronique


According to the law, **modifying an originally authentic computerized or electronic document** is a crime punishable by **two years in prison** and a **2,000-dinar fine** (Article 199 ter). An electronic document is defined as any information related to a transaction exchanged via a modern communication medium (Internet, computer, or ATM - Article 453). The **same penalty** applies to anyone who **knowingly possesses or uses** these modified documents.

## Note

for all of these offenses the penalty is doubled if the individual committed these crimes during his professional activities

# The national authority for the protection of personal data

This Body is an **independent, quasi-judicial authority** established by the **Organic Law n°2004-63** (July 27, 2004). Its specific working rules are detailed in **Decree n°2007-3003** (November 27, 2007). Although its budget is linked to the **Ministry of Justice**, its decisions can be challenged in the **Court of Appeal of Tunis** and the **Court of Cassation**, confirming its legal weight and independence.

The Body specializes in the practical regulation of data processing: it **grants authorizations** and **receives declarations** for data processing activities, and it can **revoke these permissions** as set out by law. A key responsibility is defining the **necessary guarantees** and **appropriate measures** to ensure data protection. It is also the formal recipient for **complaints** related to data protection within its legal jurisdiction.

In carrying out its mission, the Authority has two main powers:

1. **Enforcement and Access:** It is authorized to **access personal data** being processed to conduct verifications, and it can **collect any essential information** required to fulfill its duties.
2. **Advisory and Regulatory:** It gives **official opinions** on matters related to data protection law. It can **create "codes of conduct"** (rules) for how personal data should be handled. It also engages generally in all related activities, including **research, training, and studies**, that support its field of intervention.

The foundational principle for all these powers comes from **Article 24**, which states that the State is required to protect **private life, the home's inviolability, and the confidentiality of correspondence, communications, and personal data**.

# Le cadre de protection



modern technology has made it easy to attack **privacy and personal data**. Files can be **downloaded, transferred, modified, and used** quickly (often by software or search engines) to create detailed **profiles** about people (showing their usage, buying habits, statistics, etc.).

To combat this, Tunisian law establishes a strong foundation for data protection. **Article 1 of Organic Law n°2004-63** (dated July 27, 2004) clearly states that:

> **Every person has the right to have their personal data protected as a fundamental, constitutionally guaranteed right.**

This means personal data can **only be processed** with **transparency, loyalty, and respect for human dignity**, and strictly according to the law's rules.

### What is "Personal Data"?

The law broadly defines personal data as: **"Any information, no matter its source or form, that can directly or indirectly identify a natural person, making them identifiable."**

- **Identification** requires knowing a person’s identity or their **physical, psychological, genetic, physiological, social, economic, or cultural characteristics.**
- **Exception:** This definition specifically **excludes information already part of public life** or defined as such by the law.

### Legal Restrictions and Obligations

As a result of this protection, the law **forbids** a long list of actions related to personal data. This includes **any form of identification or processing** such as:

- Collecting, recording, conserving (storing), organizing, or modifying data.
- Exploiting (using), sending, distributing, spreading, destroying, or consulting data.

Any processing that _is_ allowed must **always occur with transparency and loyalty**.

### Strict Rules for Sensitive Data

The law adds an even stronger layer of protection for certain **sensitive data**. This data is legally protected and **cannot be processed** _unless_ the law specifically gives authorization. This sensitive category includes information about a person's:

- Racial or genetic origin
- Religious convictions
- Political or union opinions
- **Judicial record** (criminal history)
- **Medical file**

## Les Droits Essentiels sur Vos Données Personnelles dans la Loi Tunisienne (Loi 2004-63)


This law confirms that protecting your personal information is not just about banning things; it's about giving **you** specific, active rights over your data, both _before_ it's used and _after_ it's processed.

The rights fall into two categories:

### 1. Before They Use Your Data (Prior Rights) 

You must be informed and give permission for the processing to be legal.

#### Right to Know (Information - Art. 31)

The organization that collects your data must inform you **in advance** about what they are doing. This information must be delivered in a way that leaves a **written record**. They must tell you:

- The **purpose** and **nature** of the processing.
- **Who** is running the process (data controller) and **who** will benefit from it.
- **All your rights** (like access, correcting data, or objecting later).

This right to information is key because it allows you to **decide whether to accept or refuse** the data processing.

#### Right to Say Yes (Consent)

Your personal data can **only** be used if you give your consent. Your permission must be:

- **Prior** (given beforehand).
- **Express** (clear and unambiguous).
- **Written**.

This consent is a **condition for the processing to be legal**. If the data belongs to a **minor** (a child), their legal **guardian** or the **family judge** must be the one to give the consent.

---

### 2. After They Use Your Data (Subsequent Rights) 

You keep control over your data even after it's been collected, allowing you to check, correct, or stop its use.

#### Right to Access and Correct (Rectification)

This right gives you the ability to check the personal files they have created about you.

- **Access:** You can **consult and access** your personal file (which is defined as structured and grouped data that identifies you). You also have the right to get a **copy** of your data within **one month** of submitting your written request.
- **If Access is Denied:** If the organization refuses to give you access or the copy, you can file a request with the **INPDP** (National Data Protection Authority) within one month. The INPDP can then order the consultation or release of the information, or it can approve the refusal.
- **Preventing Damage:** You can also ask the INPDP to take "all appropriate measures" to **prevent the destruction or spreading** of your personal data (Art. 38) while your request is pending.
- **Rectification (Fixing Data - Art. 40):** You have the right to demand that your personal data be **completed, modified, clarified, updated, or even deleted (erased)**.
- **Destruction:** If the processing violated the rules of the Law 2004-63, you can request that the data be **destroyed**.

#### Right to Object

Given the sensitive nature of personal data, the law grants you a direct right to object to any processing.

- **Conditions:** This right is **not limited in time**. However, your objection must be based on **valid, legitimate, and serious reasons**.
- **Scope:** The objection can specifically apply to the sharing of your data with **third parties for advertising purposes**.
- **Result:** If you object, the consequence is the **immediate suspension of the processing**.
